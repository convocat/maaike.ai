## Summary
N. Katherine Hayles is a literary scholar and theorist known for her work on posthumanism and cognition. She theorised the concept of "cognitive assemblage" — the idea that cognition is not located in individual minds but distributed across humans, technologies, and environments. Maaike encounters her work through reading notes and uses it to challenge anthropocentric assumptions about AI.

## How Maaike uses this concept
Maaike uses Hayles's cognitive assemblage concept to argue that LLMs are not a special case but an instance of a broader pattern: cognition has always been distributed and non-conscious in part. This reframes AI not as alien intelligence but as another node in a long-running cognitive assemblage. It's a gentler, less threatening framing than the bullshitter/stochastic parrot critique.
