# Serendipity

**Type:** concept
**First seen:** 2024 (knowledge-gardens-and-serendipity)
**Last updated:** 2026-04-12

## Summary
Serendipity, in Maaike's knowledge garden context, refers to the unexpected discovery of meaningful connections — the "happy accident" of finding something relevant you weren't looking for. It is in tension with structured search and utility. Maaike asks whether a digital garden should optimize for findability or for serendipitous exploration, and comes down on the side of serendipity as a design value.

## How Maaike uses this concept
Maaike connects serendipity to her broader philosophy of non-linear knowledge: the garden as a space for wandering, not just looking things up. This is partly a reaction to her professional background in structured, hierarchical content systems. It also informs her knowledge graph project — embeddings that surface surprising connections rather than obvious ones. Serendipity is not accidental; it can be designed for.
