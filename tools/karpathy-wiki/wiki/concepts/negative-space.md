# Negative space

**Type:** design-concept
**First seen:** 2024 (saturday-design-thoughts)
**Last updated:** 2026-04-12

## Summary
Negative space, borrowed from visual design, refers to the deliberate absence of elements — the space around and between things that gives the remaining elements meaning. Maaike applies the concept to LLM interface design: the question is not only what to put in front of the user but when to show nothing. Silence, absence, and restraint are design choices.

## How Maaike uses this concept
In the context of AI interfaces, Maaike asks what it would mean to design the silence: moments when the AI does not respond, does not offer suggestions, does not fill the space. This connects directly to designing for doubt — if every interaction produces a confident output, users have no cognitive room to question it. Negative space creates that room.
