# Epistemic bias

**Type:** concept
**First seen:** 2024 (thinking-in-action-precision-matters)
**Last updated:** 2026-04-12

## Summary
Epistemic bias is a systematic tendency to evaluate information in ways that preserve existing beliefs rather than revise them in light of evidence. Maaike argues that dialectical thinking is the antidote to epistemic bias — and that LLMs actively reinforce it by generating fluent, convincing text that validates rather than challenges the user's existing framing.

## How Maaike uses this concept
Epistemic bias is the cognitive trap that makes precise thinking so difficult, and that makes LLMs particularly hazardous as thinking partners. When you prompt an LLM with your existing framing, it tends to build on it rather than challenge it. This is not neutral — it amplifies the epistemic bias you brought in. Maaike's prescription is to practice dialectical thinking deliberately: seek the counter-position, test your convictions, distinguish between conviction and mere opinion.
