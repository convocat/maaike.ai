# Conversational grounding

**Type:** concept
**First seen:** 2023 (is-conversation-still-a-useful-metaphor)
**Last updated:** 2026-04-12

## Summary
Conversational grounding is the process by which conversation partners establish and maintain mutual understanding, through grounding acts that confirm shared meaning. Theorised by Herbert Clark and Susan Brennan, it is the cooperative achievement at the heart of real conversation. Maaike uses it as the standard that LLMs fail to meet — and as evidence that the conversation metaphor is structurally misleading when applied to AI.

## How Maaike uses this concept
Maaike's argument is precise: not only do LLMs fail to achieve grounding in practice, they cannot achieve it in principle with their current mechanism. Grounding requires mutual, iterative confirmation of shared meaning — a two-way process. LLMs produce outputs that perform the surface form of conversational response without engaging in the underlying cooperative process. When users assume grounding is happening (because the interface looks like a conversation), they misplace their trust. This is a design problem: the interface should not create false expectations of grounding.
