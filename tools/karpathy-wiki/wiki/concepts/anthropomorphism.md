# Anthropomorphism

**Type:** concept
**First seen:** 2023 (is-conversation-still-a-useful-metaphor)
**Last updated:** 2026-04-12

## Summary
Anthropomorphism is the attribution of human characteristics, emotions, or intentions to non-human entities. In Maaike's work it is not a philosophical curiosity but a concrete design risk: the conversation metaphor activates anthropomorphism as a default cognitive response, leading users to attribute understanding, intention, and trustworthiness to systems that have none of these.

## How Maaike uses this concept
Maaike argues that anthropomorphism is not a user error — it is a predictable consequence of design choices. Conversational interfaces are designed to feel like talking to someone. That someone-ness triggers anthropomorphic assumptions automatically. The design risk is that users extend trust, assume grounding, and lower their critical guard based on a structural illusion. Conversation designers who build warmth and personality into agents are, in Rodemann's framing, performing emotional labor — and creating conditions for user anthropomorphism.
