# Cognitive laziness

**Type:** concept
**First seen:** 2023 (why-chatgpt-is-bullshit-and-why-we-should-design-for-that)
**Last updated:** 2026-04-12

## Summary
Cognitive laziness is the human tendency to minimize cognitive effort in communication — defaulting to trust and assuming cooperative intent rather than verifying claims. In Maaike's argument, cognitive laziness is the user-side vulnerability that makes LLM indifference to truth dangerous: users are predisposed not to check, not to verify, not to apply critical scrutiny to outputs that feel conversational and confident.

## How Maaike uses this concept
Maaike does not blame users for being cognitively lazy — she treats it as a baseline cognitive tendency that good design must account for. The interface cannot assume users will critically evaluate LLM outputs. This is precisely why designing for doubt is necessary: if you don't build skepticism into the interface, users won't supply it themselves. Cognitive laziness compounds with anthropomorphism: not only do users not check, they actively attribute trustworthiness to the system.
