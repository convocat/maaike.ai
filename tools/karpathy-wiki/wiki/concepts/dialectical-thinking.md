# Dialectical thinking

**Type:** concept
**First seen:** 2024 (thinking-in-action-precision-matters)
**Last updated:** 2026-04-12

## Summary
Dialectical thinking is a method of reasoning that develops understanding by confronting a position with its opposite, and resolving the tension into a synthesis. Attributed to Hegel and structured as thesis-antithesis-synthesis. Maaike argues it is the antidote to epistemic bias — and that LLMs, by default, work against it by confirming and elaborating rather than genuinely challenging.

## How Maaike uses this concept
Maaike frames dialectical thinking as a cognitive practice, not just an academic method. Precise thinking requires deliberately seeking the counter-position, not just sharpening your existing view. LLMs are particularly problematic here: they generate fluent, convincing text that validates your framing, making it harder to notice when you're in an epistemic bubble. Andrej Karpathy is cited as a model of dialectical thinking in practice — someone who publicly updates his positions when confronted with counter-evidence.
