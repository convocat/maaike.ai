# Articulation barrier

**Type:** concept
**First seen:** 2023 (conversational-interfaces-are-not-easy)
**Last updated:** 2026-04-12

## Summary
The articulation barrier is Nielsen Norman Group's term for the usability threshold in generative AI: to get what you want, you must first be able to express it precisely in a prompt — a skill that is not trivial. Maaike uses it as a structuring concept in her argument that conversational interfaces impose new cognitive demands, and that direct manipulation is often the better design response.

## How Maaike uses this concept
Maaike sees the articulation barrier as a fundamental limitation of the conversational interface paradigm: if users can't articulate what they want, they can't get it. This is not just about prompt writing skill — it's about the cognitive cost of externalizing tacit knowledge in linguistic form. The articulation barrier compounds with cognitive load: every conversational interaction forces you to formulate a request explicitly, even when what you want is something you'd recognize immediately if shown.
