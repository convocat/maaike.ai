Chip handwriting candidates

All shown with option E tilt (chip + text rotated at different angles). The chip border, color, and shape are constant; only the text font changes.

0. Nunito (current, for reference)

Sanity-check baseline. The chip text is the same Nunito body font as the rest of the chat.

1. Cedarville Cursive

Thin connected pen-stroke. Most "spidery" of the lot. Reads as someone in a hurry with a fine point.

2. La Belle Aurore

Thin italic cursive, more elegant than Cedarville. Slightly Victorian, like a margin note in an old letter.

3. Reenie Beanie

Thin casual print. Erratic letter heights, jaggy. Reads as scribble more than handwriting.

4. Shadows Into Light

Print-style, quick, slightly tilted naturally. Less spidery, more "felt-tip in a notebook".

5. Mrs Saint Delafield

Thin formal cursive. Very Victorian, near-calligraphic. Risks reading as "wedding invitation" rather than chat.

6. Caveat

Less spidery, more relaxed. Reads as quick marker handwriting. Included as a "warmer" alternative to compare against.

7. Indie Flower

Round, friendly print-style. Not spidery; included for contrast.

8. Architects Daughter

Even-stroke print, slightly mechanical. Architectural notebook feel.

For "spidery" specifically: Cedarville Cursive or La Belle Aurore are the closest matches. Cedarville is more legible at small sizes; La Belle Aurore is more elegant. Reenie Beanie is the most chaotic but legibility drops.
